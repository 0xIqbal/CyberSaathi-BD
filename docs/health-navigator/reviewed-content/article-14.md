ENGLISH EXPLANATION:
Typhoid fever is a bacterial infection usually spread through food or water contaminated with faeces. It can cause prolonged fever, weakness, abdominal pain, headache, constipation or diarrhoea, and loss of appetite.
DGHS lists typhoid and paratyphoid fevers among waterborne diseases in Bangladesh. A clinician should assess persistent or unexplained fever, especially if there is abdominal pain, poor intake, or possible exposure to unsafe food or water. Do not take antibiotics without a proper clinical assessment.

IMPORTANT WARNINGS:
Seek urgent care for severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, blood in stool, fainting, or inability to drink fluids. Do not stop prescribed antibiotics early or take leftover antibiotics.

NEXT STEPS:

See a qualified clinician promptly for persistent fever or unexplained illness.
Follow the clinician’s advice on blood tests, stool tests, and treatment.
Drink safe, boiled or treated water and avoid unhygienically prepared food.
Complete any prescribed antibiotic course exactly as directed.
