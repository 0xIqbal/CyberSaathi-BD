ENGLISH EXPLANATION:
Dengue is a viral infection spread by the bite of infected female Aedes mosquitoes, especially Aedes aegypti. Common symptoms include high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain, nausea, vomiting, and sometimes diarrhoea.
There is no specific home cure for dengue. A clinician should assess fever, hydration, warning signs, and whether blood tests or hospital care are needed. Do not assume every fever is dengue; other illnesses can resemble it.

IMPORTANT WARNINGS:
Seek urgent medical care for severe abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, bleeding from gums or nose, black stool, unusual bruising, extreme weakness, cold/clammy skin, confusion, convulsions, or difficulty breathing. IEDCR lists severe abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhoea, convulsions, bruising, and uncontrolled bleeding as warning features of severe dengue.

NEXT STEPS:

Contact a qualified clinician or visit a hospital if fever is high, persistent, or accompanied by warning signs.
Drink fluids as advised by a clinician; do not force large amounts of water if vomiting is severe.
Use mosquito nets, protective clothing, and approved repellents; remove standing water around the home.
Do not take aspirin, ibuprofen, or other anti-inflammatory painkillers without advice from a qualified clinician, because bleeding risk may increase.
