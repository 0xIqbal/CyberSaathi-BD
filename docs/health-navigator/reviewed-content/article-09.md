ENGLISH EXPLANATION:
Diabetes is a long-term condition in which blood glucose (sugar) remains higher than normal. In Bangladesh, diabetes prevalence has been reported as increasing over time; IEDCR cites national estimates of 5.52 percent in 2013 and 7.4 percent in 2015, while the International Diabetes Federation estimated 6.9 percent adult prevalence in 2017.
Common symptoms can include frequent urination, excessive thirst, unexplained weight loss, tiredness, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, and repeated infections. Some people have no obvious symptoms, so a clinician may recommend a blood glucose test based on risk factors, symptoms, or routine screening.

IMPORTANT WARNINGS:
Seek urgent care for confusion, fainting, severe weakness, very fast breathing, fruity-smelling breath, severe vomiting, or inability to keep fluids down—these can occur with dangerously high or low blood glucose. Do not stop prescribed diabetes medicines or insulin without advice from a qualified clinician.

NEXT STEPS:

Ask a qualified clinician whether you need a fasting blood glucose, random glucose, or HbA1c test.
Follow the individual diet, activity, medicine, and monitoring plan given by your clinician.
Check feet daily for cuts, redness, swelling, or wounds that do not heal.
Attend follow-up visits for blood pressure, kidney, eye, and foot assessment as advised.
