ENGLISH EXPLANATION:
Diarrhoea means passing loose or watery stools more often than usual. It can be caused by infections, unsafe food or water, and other illnesses. Frequent watery diarrhoea can quickly cause dehydration, especially in young children, older adults, and people who are already unwell.
Oral rehydration solution (ORS) helps replace fluid and salts lost through diarrhoea. icddr,b highlights correct ORS use, early detection of dehydration, appropriate patient management, and avoiding unnecessary antibiotics as important parts of diarrhoea care.

IMPORTANT WARNINGS:
Seek urgent medical care for signs of severe dehydration, such as very little or no urine, extreme thirst, sunken eyes, weakness, confusion, fainting, or inability to drink. Also seek urgent care for blood in stool, repeated vomiting, high fever, severe abdominal pain, or diarrhoea in a young child, older adult, or person with a serious illness.

NEXT STEPS:

Start ORS early if diarrhoea occurs, following the packet instructions or a clinician’s advice.
Continue feeding; for infants, continue breastfeeding unless a qualified clinician advises otherwise.
Wash hands with soap and water, use safe drinking water, and handle food hygienically.
Do not take antibiotics without a clinician’s advice; they do not help all types of diarrhoea and can be harmful when used unnecessarily.
